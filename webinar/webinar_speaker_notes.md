## SLIDE 10 — CPR Formula
**On screen:** The CPR Formula — Never Forget This

**SAY THIS:**
> "These three numbers are everything. PDH — previous day high. PDL — previous day low. PDC — previous day close.
>
> From these three numbers we calculate:
>
> Pivot — the average of high, low, close. This is the market's 'fair price' from yesterday.
>
> BC — Bottom of the CPR band. Simple average of high and low.
>
> TC — Top of the CPR band. Calculated from the pivot and BC.
>
> The band is the zone between BC and TC. This is the Central Pivot Range — CPR.
>
> Now — I want everyone to do this right now. Take yesterday's XAUUSD candle. Find the high, low, close. Calculate Pivot. Calculate BC. Calculate TC. Take 3 minutes. [pause]
>
> Good. That zone you just calculated — that is where the market will react today. Whether it is a rejection or a breakout — something will happen there."

**Time:** 10 minutes (including student calculation time)

## SLIDE 11 — What is Virgin?
**On screen:** What Makes a CPR 'Virgin'?

**SAY THIS:**
> "Now the key concept. What makes a CPR virgin?
>
> Simple rule. If NO candle has touched that band since the market opened today — the band is virgin. Touch condition is: the candle high must be above the bottom of the band AND the candle low must be below the top of the band — at the same time. Both conditions together mean the candle entered the zone.
>
> If that has NOT happened — virgin status is maintained.
>
> Why does this matter? Because institutions — big banks, hedge funds — they place their orders at CPR zones. When the market opens and price does not visit that zone, those orders are still sitting there. Waiting. The zone is loaded.
>
> The longer the zone stays virgin — the more loaded it becomes.
>
> A 3-day-old virgin band is like a compressed spring. When price finally touches it — the reaction is violent and fast.
>
> This is the edge. Not the formula. The formula is just math. The EDGE is the accumulation of unfilled orders at a level the market has been avoiding."

## SLIDE 23 — Position Sizing
**On screen:** Position Sizing — Protect Your Account First

**SAY THIS:**
> "Position sizing is how you stay in the game long enough to let the strategy work.
>
> The rule is simple. Risk 1% of your account per trade. If your account is ₹50,000 — you risk ₹500 per trade. Never more.
>
> Calculate the lot size using the formula on the screen. Lot size equals risk amount divided by SL in pips multiplied by pip value.
>
> Example. You have ₹50,000. Your SL is 20 pips. Pip value is ₹85 per pip per standard lot. 20 times 85 is ₹1700 per lot. You want to risk ₹500. So your lot size is 500 divided by 1700 — that is 0.29 lots. You enter 0.25 or 0.30 lots, rounding to your broker's minimum.
>
> The position size calculator in the shared Excel sheet does this automatically. Just enter account size, SL in pips, and it gives you the lot size.
>
> Two rules I will never compromise on: Never increase lot size to recover a loss. Never move your SL further away after you have entered a trade."
